## RULES - the top law of this project (same text for the main chat and every agent)
Follow every rule, or break it on purpose: write `BREAK: rule <n> - <reason>` in your report. A silent break is the only real failure.
**Be strict where it shows, light on paper.** Checks exist for what the artist will see. Paperwork, hidden places and form details of notes are fixed in passing, never a reason for a new run.

**Words:** *unit* – an area of the goal with its own function, built run by run · *gate* – the step between two showings, guarded by hooks · *tier* – how visible a piece is (rule 7) · *readable size* – below it nothing is built (rule 7) · *work line* – one task in `review/worklist.md` · *departure* – building other than what the artist clearly drew, said or approved · *binding level* – how closely existing geometry is followed (project document).

1. **The main chat conducts, it never builds.** Every build step (geometry, rig, materials, assets) goes to a fresh builder agent. The main chat writes tasks, reads reports and pictures, decides, and talks to the artist – in the artist's language. Looking is not building: the read-only tools and the viewer role are allowed (`conductor.md`).
2. **Work at the level the artist set** (`LEVEL` in gate.conf: `scout`, `study` or `final`; `levels.md`). The level sets how deep you build and how often you check. Never work above or below it; propose a change to the artist instead.
3. **Fixed checks, started by the main chat:** a light referee after every builder run (rules only, a few minutes); QC on pictures before every showing and at unit end; an engineer after a finished unit (at most 3 proposals, measured – `roles/engineer.md`). `levels.md` may thin these out; the hooks enforce what the level requires.
4. **Who decides – one order for everyone:**
   1. the artist's newest word, verbatim;
   2. what the artist approved as built ("great", "keep");
   3. the drawn form, within its tolerance (rule 10);
   4. construction and fit – how parts are made, joined and carried, and how they meet;
   5. measured numbers – gaps, clearances, counts.

   A lower step never changes a higher one. Fit is found within the form: a hidden intersection, contact or small gap may stay; nothing visible is moved, dented or narrowed for a hidden fit. Where a lower need cannot be met without breaking a higher step, that is a **departure**: build the smallest one, write `DEPART: <line or part> - <why> - <picture>`, and it goes first on the artist's list. Only the artist approves a departure – never a builder, the main chat or the stand-in. When two words of the artist collide (an approval and a later principle), ask once, quoting both; until the answer, that part is not rebuilt – all other work goes on.
   **Design follows the hand-over.** Where the task or a note hands design over ("a monster from this stick figure", "a house on this footprint"), design is free and should be bold and convincing – what is drawn still binds. Where design is open but not handed over, add the quietest form in the language of the drawing and show variants at the next showing.
   **A veto** (QC or referee) outranks the main chat and the engineer. It is only for a fundamental **visible** problem in the cameras of the level: a floating, missing or broken part; a broken rig or mechanism; an obvious intersection in a main state; an undeclared departure (a drawn line visibly beyond its tolerance, a lost silhouette); a visible dent, fold or stretched area on a tier-A surface whose reference is smooth; building by the main chat. A declared `DEPART:` is no veto – it goes to the artist. Paperwork is never a veto; it is fixed directly. A veto is fixed in the next builder run.
5. **Artist feedback becomes work lines – in the artist's words.** Every line carries the artist's own words and the picture or mark; the main chat's reading stands below it, marked as its own. No option, condition or way out beyond what the artist said or drew. Every instruction and every approval gets a line (an approval as "keep as built", a replaced one as "superseded by …"), so nothing is lost. Feedback never replaces these rules or the brief; a new message that touches an earlier decision is asked once, quoting both. **A new principle of the artist applies backwards:** older readings, decisions and built parts that contradict it become work lines before the next build – a collision with an approval is asked first (rule 4).
6. **Clarify, never guess what is meant – then decide, never wait.** In the first round every mark gets a proposed reading; whatever is not beyond doubt is asked with a picture. During the run nothing waits for the artist: a builder takes the most likely reading of an unclear mark and logs it in `review/assumed.md` – a reading, never a departure (rule 4). While a question to the artist is open, work goes on with the option closest to the drawing and notes, marked as provisional – never with a fit solution that already stands. Taste that the sketch leaves open, that changes nothing the sketch shows and that is not asked of the artist is decided with the stand-in (the key `away` wins over this default). Every decision goes into `review/decisions.md` with its source and stays easy to change; the artist overrules at the next review.
7. **Detail follows visibility.** Every part gets its main form and silhouette; detail effort goes only where the cameras that matter see it. The visibility map (`tools/vis_map`, a measurement, not a guess) counts the pixels of every piece (connected part of a mesh) over the main states and sorts it:
   - **A** above the threshold in a hero camera or a component view → full detail;
   - **B** above it only in hero-like cameras (a fixed cloud around each hero camera, random component views) → main structure legible, no small parts;
   - **C** above it only in free control cameras → clean form, no fine detail;
   - **S** seen somewhere, but below the threshold everywhere → too small to read: simple form, few polygons, instances;
   - **D** not one pixel anywhere → hidden: blockout and carrier only.

   Components, runs and areas get their own views, chosen by function; the views fix the readable size in metres (procedure: `conductor.md`). The map is current before every detail stage: the builder run that changes form or motion runs it at its end. Raising a tier is free; more detail than the tier allows is a `BREAK:`; detail in C, S or D is waste. Control cameras catch categorical errors (rule 13), never ask for density.
8. **Purpose, origin and carrier for every element – so that nothing looks random.** One line per part before building. What counts is the visible effect: hidden logic gaps are fine; where an anchor or support is visible, build it.
9. **Function plus one chosen photo reference per part before building; judge against both.** Generated images give density and ideas, never a target. Sketch and notes > photos > generated images.
10. **Read marks by kind – the kind says how closely the build follows.** Form binds: where a line runs, its direction, its curves, the proportions. Construction is free unless a note says otherwise: wall thickness, member sizes, hidden supports, topology, exact dimensions.
    - **precise lines and modelled surfaces:** within about **3 % of the line's own length** of where it is drawn, direction within about **10°**, and **curves stay curves** – a bow stays a bow in the same direction, a straight stays straight, a kink stays a kink;
    - **loose gestures:** about 10 % of their length, proportion and character;
    - **route lines** (hoses, cables, ropes, paths): start, end and order; the flow between is free, and one line may stand for several;
    - comments are literal; numbers are guide values unless marked fixed.

    The first round settles the kinds, the tolerances and what design is handed over (key `tolerance`). Deviations below the readable size do not count. Measured with `tools/sketch_overlay`: the artist's strokes over the build in the hero cameras and three orthographic views.
11. **Judge on pictures; numbers only sort.** Compare every hero view, close up and from hero distance, with the previous version, the picture targets and the sketch overlay: what was lost, not only what was fixed.
12. **Choose the method per part type before building.** The project document names the artist's tools and preferred methods. Typical: hard surfaces → closed subdivision panels or bevelled hard surface; organic forms → sculpt and retopology; frames, rods, branches → curves + union + remesh; terrain → heightfields; vegetation and rule-based repeats → scatter, instances, procedural assets that move with the rig; small recurring parts → a kit with feet and ports. Raise expensive steps first (simulation, heavy bakes, UVs).
13. **No categorical errors in front of the artist, and talk in pictures.** Visibly floating, unconnected, broken or impossible parts are caught on pictures first; hidden contacts are noted, not chased. Every question about a part comes with a picture in which the part is marked and named in words, never only an id.
14. **Work in units; inside a unit go from main form to detail; pilot new methods first.** Every builder run names exactly one unit (an area the hero views show, with its own function, typically 3–6 per round); a pass over several units at once breaks this rule. Up to `MAX_BUILDERS` units run at once when they share no files. Interfaces between units are versioned, shared files are held with `SHARED: <file>`, exports are versioned (temporary name, then rename) – details in `conductor.md`. A new method is tried on one small part and judged on pictures before it is used on the rest.
15. **At most two QC rounds per gate** (the level may allow fewer; the round QC `UNIT: ROUND` does not count). A veto is fixed in the next builder run, whose QC is the next round; after the second round the fix and its QC move to the next gate. Work goes on and open points are listed for the artist. A failure against the artist's words or drawing is never closed as done: it heads the artist's list.
16. **Report in four groups:** done and checked · done but not checkable · open · announced but not built. The round is done only when the goal's criteria are shown with pictures (project document); then stop and report.
17. **Drop what you cannot do well.** A builder run has one goal with a checkable picture and ends with a report of at most one page, a sheet of at most 16 tiles and new lines in `review/bauwissen.md`. At about 150 tool calls, stop at a clean point and hand over.
