**Reading notes:** a note points at one or a few example spots. It often means the whole part and its similar or mirrored counterparts (pointing at three of six identical joints, windows or legs means all six), unless its text says otherwise; where it may be a one-off (damage, a scar, a single special detail), ask. Each anchor below names the object it hits and has a picture (`views/note_N#_k.png`) showing that object whole and highlighted, with a line from the note's name to the spot. Where the artist marked further parts the note also means, the anchor lists them as „also meant“ (lighter in its picture; `part_notes` in `brief.json` lists every part with the notes that reach it). That list is marked by hand and may be incomplete – similar parts are likely meant too. A picture's caption („…“) can name the parts the picture belongs to („→ parts H3, M5“, confirmed by the artist); `part_notes` lists such pictures at the part too. Each note board comes as one picture (whole board) and in sections – frames the artist drew (titled), everything else grouped by proximity; each section has a picture showing how text, images and hand-drawn arrows belong together. Arrows are numbered P#: the list names what they connect, the section picture shows exactly where. Viewport screenshots show the sketch itself (their cameras are in `brief.json`). Arrows often only point in a direction – read text and picture together, and ask when unsure.

**How to read the sketch:**
- Existing geometry (meshes, surfaces) is the starting point, unless set otherwise on single parts: its silhouette, proportions, positions, how the parts connect and the axes of motion hold; develop the rest – main bodies, cross-sections, transitions, connections – rather than only adding detail. What looks like a by-product of low resolution or quick modelling (facets, small kinks, stray waves) is not intent: where the form is meant to be smooth, smooth it; where its character is irregular (bark, rock, skin, grown or worn forms), keep that character, not the facets. Ask when unsure.
- Lines, marks and sketches over images show intent – roughly where, and what character – not exact dimensions. Exact positions follow from the existing geometry and from how the part works or sits.
- Words like smooth, flowing, flush or calmer are design goals, not measurements. Where the artist gives numbers, they matter.
- Where something moves or deforms: think through every state (e.g. open, closed, folded, posed, the extremes) and what touches or collides, even when no note mentions it.
